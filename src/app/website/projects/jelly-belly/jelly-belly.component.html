Jelly Belly: Bean Appétit

Jelly Belly prepared for a complete website transformation, accompanied by a national campaign that shifted its positioning towapd a more premium product.

Key Objectives

  1. Bring the “Bean Appetit” website to life—a new brand concept that celebrates the art of joyful indulgence, positioning sweets as both premium and fun.
  2. Simplify navigation across an extensive and evolving product portfolio.
  3. Highlight key product lines and collections without overwhelming users.
  4. Develop a modular system that can easily scale for updates in 2026-2027.
  5. Blend storytelling with discovery to create a more human and emotionally engaging experience.
  6. Preserve the brand's playful essence by elevating existing “fun” content and sections into a more cohesive, premium narrative experience.

1. The Starting Point

To position itself as a premium product with a carefully crafted flavor profile, the brand needed to convey this through its digital platform; however, the existing site still reflected its previous identity and failed to meet certain standards and objectives that Jelly Belly had set for itself as a brand.

Problems to Solve:

  • Outdated visual identity, lacking consistency across sections.
  • Communication tone that did not align with the brand's new direction.
  • Pages built without a coherent design system, making it difficult to scale content.

2. The Challenge

The campaign was developed in parallel with the website. During the design phase, we did not have full access to the commercials or the final photography—neither the framing, the definitive color palettes, nor the duration of the videos.

The question shifted from "what does the site look like?" to:

How do we design a complete site that functions on its own and remains consistent with any material that might be added to it?

3. How do we work as a team?

  • We defined shared design principles.
  • We divided the site into areas (commerce and collections, editorial content, interactive experiences) and assigned a lead for each.
  • We set up synchronization meetings with the agency to review preliminary drafts and references before the final assets were delivered.
  • We conducted design reviews and QA in parallel, rather than waiting until the end.

I coordinated a 5-person UX/UI team that redesigned the entire site—not just the homepage. Each workstream operated in parallel, utilizing a shared design system, while constant synchronization with the creative agency allowed us to move forward without waiting for final campaign assets. I also maintained ongoing communication with the development team throughout every handoff.

4. Three principles we established to define the website.

1. Design for the container, not for the image. Each component adapts to any photo or video.

2. The platform must be able to stand on its own. If a campaign is delayed or changes, the site continues to function and looks good.

3. One voice, many touchpoints. The tone of the campaign also defines the interface and microcopy—not just the imagery.

5. Wireframes

Before defining color or typography, we redesigned the structure of the entire site. We began with a sitemap and the primary navigation flows, then built low-fidelity wireframes for each screen: home, collections, product, editorial content, recipes, interactive experiences, and points of sale.

Some ideas were discarded at this stage; this helped us better understand the client and determine the direction for developing creative UI proposals.

6. Creative proposal

  • The campaign: an interchangeable theme that activates a specific color palette and campaign direction without requiring a rebuild of the pages.
  • Collections as a tasting menu: a navigation experience that presents each collection as an immersive journey rather than a mere catalog.
  • Refined visual style: from the choice of typography to the layout of sections and animations throughout the website.

Several creative concepts were explored with the goal of finding the right path to align with the brand's new renaissance; some ideas were adopted, while others served as learning experiences throughout the visual exploration process.

7. Handoff For DEV

During the handoff to development, my responsibility was to ensure that the design reached production exactly as intended, and that every visual decision had a clear technical translation.

What did I deliver to the development team?

  • A component library covering all states (normal, hover, focus, disabled, error, loading).
  • Design tokens with naming conventions aligned with their implementation in code.
  • Responsive specifications for each breakpoint, including aspect ratios, image cropping, and video logic for both desktop and mobile.
  • Content rules: character limits, and how to handle long titles or missing images.
  • Accessibility requirements for each component, such as contrast and alternative text.

I worked closely with the development team to align components, tokens, and responsive behavior before any code was written, and I reviewed the implementation with the creative director once it had been built.

8. Outcome & impact

We delivered a modern, highly adaptable digital platform that unifies the aesthetic of the new campaign with a refreshed visual identity. The redesign encompassed the entire site, and the design system allowed for the seamless integration of all future assets, resulting in a fluid brand experience.

9. Lessons Learned

Designing amidst uncertainty taught me that a robust system doesn't depend on having everything defined, but rather on clearly defining the rules. I also learned that aligning an entire team around a shared system is what makes it possible to scale a redesign across an entire site without compromising brand consistency.
